Why Pulse exists

Group chats made it easy to talk. Pulse makes it easy to actually hang out.

Pulse lives in the moment before a plan exists: the “I’d be down” you’d never say out loud. It quietly finds the overlap, takes the awkwardness out of asking, then gets out of the way so you can go do the thing.

Intent before logistics

Start with what you’d be down for, not a calendar invite. Times stay loose until there’s actually a plan.

Nobody has to go first

“Still down?” is private. Nobody ever sees a no, so saying yes costs nothing.

Built to get you out

No feed to scroll, no likes to chase. Pulse counts plans that happened, not time in the app.

Twenty seconds a week

Say it like you’d text it.

Hold the circle and say what you’re up for. Pulse turns “down for food this afternoon” into a loose signal on your week, and checks it against your friends right away.

  • Talk or tap. Voice, a quick pick, or drag a window on your week.
  • Fuzzy on purpose. “After class” and “Friday night” are real answers here.
  • Blank space is fine. One signal for the whole week is plenty.

How it works

From “I’d be down” to “we’re actually doing this.”

Five quick moments. Pulse does the awkward parts in between.

1 Signal

Set your Pulse

Pick a vibe (eat, move, go out, chill, study, anything) and roughly when. Keep it open to friends, or keep it quiet until someone’s a match.

“I’d be down.”

2 Overlap

Pulse finds the overlap

Pulse quietly spots when friends want the same kind of thing at the same time: lunch, a run, a night out, a study sesh. No polls, no calendar grids.

“There might be something here.”

3 Check

Still down?

Close to the time, Pulse privately asks each person if they’re still in. Tap yes or no. Nobody sees a no, and nobody has to go first.

4 Match

It’s a Pulse

Two yeses and it’s on. Everyone who’s in finds out at the same moment. It lands on your week like a ticket.

“We’re actually in.”

5 Pulse room

Make it happen

A short-lived room opens just for the people who are in. Pick a spot and go. It clears itself two weeks later, because your group chat already exists.

That happened
