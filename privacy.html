PRIVACY POLICY

Your privacy, in plain words.

Last updated September 30, 2026

Pulse helps friends turn “I’d be down” into real plans. We collect only what that takes. Here’s what we collect, why, who can see it, and the choices you have.

No adsNever soldNo trackersDelete anytime
We keep it light

Your name, the rough times you’re down, and who your friends are. That’s the heart of it.

Quiet means quiet

Quiet signals and your “Still down?” answers stay private until a Pulse actually forms.

Friends by invite only

Pulse never reads your contacts or asks for your phone number. Friends join with a code or a link you share.

You’re in control

Edit or delete anything, choose your notifications, and delete your account from Settings.

Who we are

Pulse (“Pulse”, “we”, “us”) is an app made by an independent developer based in California, USA. This policy covers the Pulse app and the services behind it.

Questions about your privacy? Email pulse.echo.support@gmail.com. A real person reads every message.

What we collect and why

How you sign in

Why: With Apple: a sign-in token, plus the name and email Apple shares (you can hide your email with Apple’s relay address). With Google: your name, email address and profile picture, only to create your account. We never see your Apple or Google password.

Who can see it: No other user.

Profile: first name, optional last name, school or group, color, and optional photo

Why: So friends recognize you.

Who can see it: Friends, people with a pending friend request, and members of your clubs and Pulse rooms.

Signals: a vibe (like Food or Going Out), a rough time window, Open or Quiet, friends or a club, and whether you’re “really trying”

Why: To find times that line up with your friends and club members.

Who can see it: Open: your friends, or that club’s members for club-only times. Quiet: nobody, until someone compatible also signals and Pulse asks “Still down?”. Club match partners may see a time you’re both open, never your Quiet signals.

Your answer to “Still down?”

Why: So a Pulse only forms when enough people say yes.

Who can see it: Nobody. Once a Pulse forms, its members see who’s in.

Pulse room messages

Why: So the group can coordinate.

Who can see it: Members of that room.

Friends, requests, blocks, clubs and club roles

Why: To run Pulse and keep you safe.

Who can see it: The other person in each friendship. Club members see the member list and who the admins are. Blocks are never shown to anyone.

Club details: name, description, emoji, color and photo

Why: So members recognize their club.

Who can see it: Club members. Anyone with the club’s code sees its name, picture and size before joining.

Notification choices and your device’s push token

Why: To send only the notifications you want.

Who can see it: No other user.

“Did it happen?” answers

Why: To learn whether Pulse helps plans actually happen.

Who can see it: Nobody. We only look at totals.

Reports you send

Why: To review safety problems, including a reported message’s text.

Who can see it: Our team only.

Time zone and basic technical logs (like IP address and request times) kept by our hosting provider

Why: To send prompts at sensible local times, and for security and troubleshooting.

Who can see it: Our team and hosting provider only.

Voice

When you hold the Pulse circle and speak, your phone’s built-in speech recognition turns your words into text (Apple may process the audio under Apple’s privacy policy). Pulse uses that text to fill in a signal on your phone. We don’t record audio or store what you said, only the signal you save.

Pulse Pod invite links

Each Pod member gets their own invite link, so we know who invited whom and can tell them when someone joins. The invite page shows the club’s name, picture and member count, and the inviter’s first name, to anyone with the link. It never lists members.

If you open an invite link on your phone before you have Pulse, the page saves a scrambled code (a salted hash) made from your network address and basic phone details like screen size, time zone, language and iOS version. After you install Pulse and sign in, the app makes the same code to hand you the invite. We never store the address itself, and we delete these codes within a day.

Notifications

Pulse sends a small number of notifications: “Still down?” checks (normally at most three a day), new Pulses, Pair Pulse matches and times, friend requests, room messages, club news, and a weekly “Set your Pulse” reminder.

Choose which ones you get in Settings & privacy → Notifications, or pause them all. Muted notifications still wait for you in your in-app inbox.

Who we share with

We don’t sell your personal information, share it for advertising, or use third-party analytics or tracking. A few service providers process data only to run Pulse for us:

  • Supabase hosts our database, file storage and servers.
  • Expo hosts the Pulse Pod invite web page.
  • Expo and Apple deliver push notifications.
  • Apple provides Sign in with Apple, and Google provides Sign in with Google.

We may also disclose information when the law requires it, to protect someone’s safety, or as part of a merger or sale of Pulse, in which case this policy keeps applying to your data.

How long we keep it

  • Signals are deleted 30 days after their time window ends.
  • Room messages are deleted 14 days after the Pulse ends.
  • Notifications are deleted after 30 days.
  • Scrambled codes from invite links are deleted within a day.
  • Photos are deleted when you remove or replace them.
  • Everything else is kept until you delete your account.

Deleting your account

Go to Me → Settings & privacy → Delete account. This immediately deletes your profile, photo, signals, friendships, club memberships, messages and responses. Clubs you started stay with their other members. Copies in our hosting provider’s encrypted backups expire on their normal schedule, typically within 30 days.

Your choices and rights

You can edit your profile, delete signals, remove or block people, choose your notifications, and delete your account right in the app.

Depending on where you live (for example California, the EU or the UK), you may have the right to access, correct, delete or get a copy of your data, and to object to or limit some uses of it. Email us to use these rights. We’ll respond within 30 days and won’t treat you differently for asking.

California residents: we don’t sell or share personal information, as California law defines those terms.

Age

Pulse is for adults 18 and older. We don’t knowingly collect data from anyone younger. If you believe someone under 18 is using Pulse, email us and we’ll delete the account.

Security

Data is encrypted in transit. Database rules limit each person to exactly the data described above, and our own access is limited to what’s needed to run and protect Pulse. No system is perfect, so if you notice anything off, please tell us.

Changes to this policy

If we change this policy in a meaningful way, we’ll tell you in the app before the change takes effect. The date at the top shows when it was last updated.
